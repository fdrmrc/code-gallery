\chapter{A Discontinuous Galerkin solver for the Poisson problem on general polytopal meshes generated through mesh agglomeration }
\hypertarget{index}{}\label{index}\index{A Discontinuous Galerkin solver for the Poisson problem on general polytopal meshes generated through mesh agglomeration@{A Discontinuous Galerkin solver for the Poisson problem on general polytopal meshes generated through mesh agglomeration}}

This program solves a Poisson problem on an agglomerated polytopal mesh using a symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (SIPG) method. Agglomerates are constructed by an R-\/tree based spatial indexing strategy, following the approach proposed in \+[2]\+. In addition, a graph-\/based METIS partitioner is also provided in this program for comparison.\hypertarget{index_autotoc_md1}{}\doxysection{\texorpdfstring{Running the code:\+}{Running the code:\+}}\label{index_autotoc_md1}

We consider the Poisson problem in a bounded, simply connected domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d $, $d = 2,3$. The strong formulation reads    \begin{align*}  -\Delta u &= f  && \text{in } \Omega, \\
           u &= u_D && \text{on } \partial\Omega,
\end{align*} where the right-\/hand side satisfies $f \in L^2(\Omega) $ and the prescribed Dirichlet data satisfy $u_D \in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega) $.

The corresponding weak formulation is:\+ find $u \in H^1(\Omega)$ with $u = u_D $ on $\partial\Omega $ such that     \begin{equation}  \int_{\Omega} \nabla u \cdot \nabla v \,\mathrm d\mathbf{x}
  =\int_{\Omega} f\, v \,\mathrm d\mathbf{x}
  \qquad \text{for all } v \in H_0^1(\Omega).
\end{equation}\hypertarget{index_autotoc_md4}{}\doxysection{\texorpdfstring{SIPG discretization on agglomerated polytopal meshes:\+}{SIPG discretization on agglomerated polytopal meshes:\+}}\label{index_autotoc_md4}
We discretize the weak formulation by a symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (SIPG) method on the agglomerated polytopal mesh $\mathcal{T}_h $, whose elements $K \in \mathcal{T}_h $ are mutually disjoint open polygons (for $d=2 $) or polyhedra (for $d=3 $). For each element we denote its diameter by   \[  h_K := \operatorname{diam}(K).
\] The mesh skeleton is given by   \[  \Gamma := \bigcup_{K \in \mathcal{T}_h} \partial K,
\] and we denote by $\Gamma_{\mathrm{int}} $ the union of interior faces, while $\Gamma_{\mathrm D} := \Gamma \cap \partial\Omega $ collects the Dirichlet boundary faces.

The discrete space $V_h $ consists of element-\/wise polynomials of degree at most \$p\$ on each $K \in \mathcal{T}_h $. For $u_h, v_h \in V_h $ we use the broken gradient $\nabla_h $ and the standard jump and average operators $[\![\cdot]\!]$ and $\{\!\!\{\cdot\}\!\!\}$ on faces.

The DG formulation reads:\+ find $u_h \in V_h $ such that    \begin{equation}  B(u_h,v_h) = l(v_h)
  \qquad \forall\, v_h \in V_h,
\end{equation} with                \begin{align*}  B(u_h,v_h)
  &=
  \int_{\Omega} \nabla_h u_h \cdot \nabla_h v_h \,\mathrm d\mathbf{x}
  \\
  &\quad
  - \int_{\Gamma}
    \Bigl(
      \{\!\!\{\nabla u_h\}\!\!\} \cdot [\![v_h]\!]
      +
      \{\!\!\{\nabla v_h\}\!\!\} \cdot [\![u_h]\!]
    \Bigr)\,\mathrm d s
  \\
  &\quad
  + \int_{\Gamma} \sigma \,[\![u_h]\!] \cdot [\![v_h]\!] \,\mathrm d s,
\end{align*} and        \begin{equation}  l(v_h)
  =
  \int_\Omega f\, v_h \,\mathrm d\mathbf{x}
  +
  \int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm D}}
    u_D \bigl(\sigma v_h - \nabla v_h \cdot \mathbf n\bigr)\,\mathrm d s.
\end{equation}

The penalty parameter is chosen as         \begin{equation}  \sigma(\mathbf x) = C_\sigma
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{p^2}{h_K}, &
      \text{if } \mathbf x \in \partial K \cap \partial\Omega, \\[0.5em]
    \dfrac{p^2}{\min\{h_K^+,h_K^-\}}, &
      \text{if } \mathbf x \in \Gamma_{\mathrm{int}},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation} where $h_K^\pm $ are the diameters of the two elements sharing the interior face, \$p\$ is the polynomial degree, and we fix $C_\sigma = 10 $ in this program.

This scheme is well posed and admits optimal-\/order a priori error estimates. More precisely, assuming that $u|_K \in H^{s+1}(K) $ for all $K \in \mathcal{T}_h $ and some $1 \le s \le p $, there exists a constant $C > 0 $, independent of $h $, such that    \[  \|u - u_h\|_{L^2(\Omega)}
  \le C\, h^{s+1} \, |u|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)},
\] and    \[  \|\nabla(u - u_h)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}
  \le C\, h^{s} \, |u|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)}.
\] We refer to\texorpdfstring{$\sim$}{\string~}\+[1]\+ for details of the analysis.\hypertarget{index_autotoc_md5}{}\doxysection{\texorpdfstring{Agglomeration strategies}{Agglomeration strategies}}\label{index_autotoc_md5}
Agglomeration is a key ingredient for constructing polytopic meshes. This program supports two strategies for generating agglomerates, corresponding to the choices {\ttfamily metis} and {\ttfamily rtree}. In this example, we mainly focus on the {\ttfamily rtree} strategy, which is the method developed and introduced in the work \+[2]\+.\hypertarget{index_autotoc_md6}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{R-\/tree-\/based agglomeration}{R-\/tree-\/based agglomeration}}\label{index_autotoc_md6}
\hypertarget{index_autotoc_md7}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Basic idea and data structure}{Basic idea and data structure}}\label{index_autotoc_md7}
In the {\ttfamily rtree} option, axis-\/aligned bounding boxes of all fine cells are inserted into a spatial R-\/tree. Agglomerates are obtained by grouping the cells whose bounding boxes belong to the same node at a user-\/selected extraction level of the tree. This purely geometric strategy does not require external graph partitioners and is typically fast and scalable. The number and shape of the agglomerates are determined by the R-\/tree structure and the chosen level.

This approach is particularly suitable for multilevel methods, where a {\itshape nested} agglomerated hierarchy is desirable.

At the data-\/structure level, we distinguish leaf nodes and internal nodes:\+


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item {\bfseries{Leaf nodes}} store the geometric objects (here, mesh cells or their bounding boxes).
\item {\bfseries{Internal nodes}} store:\+
\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item a pointer (or reference) to a child node,
\item a bounding box that encloses all entries contained in that child subtree.
\end{DoxyItemize}
\end{DoxyItemize}

As a result, each internal node represents a spatial grouping of the objects below it.\hypertarget{index_autotoc_md8}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Design targets}{Design targets}}\label{index_autotoc_md8}
The R-\/tree is used here as a geometry-\/aware structure for grouping cell bounding boxes. The main design targets are:\+


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item {\bfseries{Minimize box area}}:\+ reduce the area covered by each bounding box,
\item {\bfseries{Minimize overlap}}:\+ reduce overlap between neighboring boxes,
\item {\bfseries{Improve compactness}}:\+ reduce box perimeters (equivalently, favor more compact boxes).
\end{DoxyItemize}

These criteria improve the spatial quality of the hierarchy and typically lead to better grouping and query behavior.

 \hypertarget{index_autotoc_md9}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Agglomeration extraction}{Agglomeration extraction}}\label{index_autotoc_md9}
Given a collection of cells (or cut-\/cell bounding boxes), we:\+


\begin{DoxyEnumerate}
\item build an R-\/tree from their bounding boxes,
\item choose a target tree level,
\item recursively descend each node at that level to its leaf descendants,
\item agglomerate leaf cells that share the same ancestor.
\end{DoxyEnumerate}

This produces a sequence of nested agglomerated meshes, which can be used in multilevel solvers and preconditioners.

For the agglomeration workflow considered here, the R-\/tree-\/based extraction has the following practical features:\+


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item {\bfseries{Level-\/independent extraction cost (observed)}}:\+ the wall-\/clock time is approximately constant with respect to the chosen extraction level.
\item {\bfseries{Boost.\+Geometry backend}}:\+ the implementation relies on the {\ttfamily Boost.\+Geometry} R-\/tree.
\item {\bfseries{Custom traversal logic}}:\+ hierarchy traversal in the form needed for agglomeration is not directly exposed, so a custom node visitor is implemented.
\end{DoxyItemize}

These properties make the R-\/tree approach convenient for constructing nested agglomerated meshes in multilevel finite element and DG settings.\hypertarget{index_autotoc_md10}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Algorithmic pipeline}{Algorithmic pipeline}}\label{index_autotoc_md10}
Given a collection of fine-\/level cells (or cut-\/cell bounding boxes), we proceed as follows:\+


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item {\bfseries{Step 1:\+ Build the R-\/tree}} Construct an R-\/tree from the set of bounding boxes associated with the fine-\/level cells.
\item {\bfseries{Step 2:\+ Select a target level}} Choose a tree level \$l\textbackslash{}in \{1,\textbackslash{}dots,L\}\$ to control the agglomeration granularity.
\item {\bfseries{Step 3:\+ Collect leaf descendants}} For each node on level \$l\$, recursively traverse its children until leaf nodes are reached.
\item {\bfseries{Step 4:\+ Agglomerate by common ancestor}} Merge leaf cells that belong to the same subtree (same ancestor at level \$l\$).
\end{DoxyItemize}

This yields a nested hierarchy with natural parent-\/child relations across levels.

Using an R-\/tree in the agglomeration pipeline has two practical advantages:\+


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item it provides a natural hierarchy over the fine-\/level mesh entities,
\item it enables geometry-\/aware grouping through recursive subtree extraction.
\end{DoxyItemize}

This is the key mechanism used later to build nested agglomerated meshes for multilevel methods and DG discretizations.

The construction of an R-\/tree spatial index on an arbitrary fine grid provides a natural and efficient agglomeration strategy with the following features:\+


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item fully automated, robust, and dimension-\/independent;
\item it produces a balanced and nested hierarchy of agglomerates;
\item the shape of the agglomerates closely follows their axis-\/aligned bounding boxes.
\end{DoxyItemize}

These properties make the R-\/tree approach an attractive alternative to graph-\/based agglomeration methods (see \+[3]\+ for more details).\hypertarget{index_autotoc_md13}{}\doxysection{\texorpdfstring{References}{References}}\label{index_autotoc_md13}
